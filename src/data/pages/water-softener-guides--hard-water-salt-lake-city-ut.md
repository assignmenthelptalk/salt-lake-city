## What Do Salt Lake City Homeowners Need to Know About Hard Water?

Three questions cover the basics: what hard water is, why Salt Lake City water is hard and how hard it measures.

<div class="svc-grid">

<div class="svc-card">


![What Is Hard Water? Salt Lake City UT](/placeholders/card.svg)

### What Is Hard Water?

Hard water contains dissolved calcium and magnesium. It is measured in milligrams per liter (mg/L) or grains per gallon (gpg), where 1 gpg equals about 17.1 mg/L. The USGS rates water under 60 mg/L as soft, 61 to 120 mg/L as moderately hard, 121 to 180 mg/L as hard and over 180 mg/L as very hard.

</div>

<div class="svc-card">


![Why Is Salt Lake City Water Hard? Salt Lake City UT](/placeholders/card.svg)

### Why Is Salt Lake City Water Hard?

Salt Lake City water is hard because it carries minerals from the mountains and the valley aquifer. SLCDPU draws on Wasatch Range streams (City Creek, Parleys Creek, Big Cottonwood Creek and Little Cottonwood Creek) and adds well water in summer, and its report lists calcium and magnesium as naturally occurring, from the erosion of natural deposits.

</div>

<div class="svc-card">


![How Hard Is Salt Lake City Water? Salt Lake City UT](/placeholders/card.svg)

### How Hard Is Salt Lake City Water?

SLCDPU's 2026 report lists total hardness by source: 136 mg/L (8.0 gpg) at Big Cottonwood, 175 mg/L (10.2 gpg) at Parleys, 212 mg/L (12.4 gpg) at City Creek, 154 to 571 mg/L (9.6 to 33.4 gpg) in SLC wells and 184.1 mg/L (about 10.8 gpg) in Jordan Valley district water. The report gives no city-wide average, so your tap reading depends on the blend that serves your street.

</div>

</div>

## What Hard Water Does to a Home

Hard water leaves scale in water heaters, dishwashers, washing machines, pipes and fixtures, and it reacts with soap to leave a film on skin, hair and laundry. Scale narrows pipes over time and makes appliances work harder.

## Solutions for Hard Water

A [salt-based](/water-softener-guides/salt-vs-salt-free-water-softener/) water softener removes the hardness minerals, a salt-free conditioner changes how scale forms, and a filter does not address hardness. For the strongest result in water above 180 mg/L, a correctly sized salt-based softener is the standard answer. For installation, see [Water Softener Installation in Salt Lake City, UT](/water-softener-installation/).

> **Not a good fit if...** your tap tests under 60 mg/L. That water is soft, and a softener adds no benefit.

## Frequently Asked Questions

<div class="faq-list">

<details class="faq-item">
<summary>Is Salt Lake City water hard?</summary>

Yes. SLCDPU's 2026 report lists total hardness of 136 to 212 mg/L at its surface water plants and 154 to 571 mg/L in its wells, which is hard to very hard on the USGS scale.

</details>

<details class="faq-item">
<summary>Is hard water bad for your health?</summary>

Hard water is not a health risk in the amounts found in Salt Lake City. It affects plumbing, appliances, skin and laundry rather than drinking safety.

</details>

<details class="faq-item">
<summary>Why is my neighbor's water harder than mine?</summary>

Different streets receive different blends of surface water, wells and wholesale supply. SLCDPU's own sources range from 136 to 571 mg/L, so neighboring streets can differ.

</details>

<details class="faq-item">
<summary>Does boiling remove water hardness?</summary>

Boiling precipitates some of the temporary hardness as scale, and it does not make hard water soft. A softener removes the minerals before they reach the pipes.
