## How Does a Salt-Based Water Softener Work?

A salt-based softener passes water through resin beads that swap calcium and magnesium for sodium or potassium. The unit regenerates with a brine rinse when the resin fills, so water leaving the tank is soft. It gives the strongest result in water above 180 mg/L, which covers SLCDPU's City Creek plant (198 mg/L), Jordan Valley supply (189.4 mg/L) and its wells (up to 487 mg/L).

## How Does a Salt-Free Conditioner Work?

A salt-free conditioner changes the structure of the hardness minerals so they cling less to pipes and fixtures. The minerals stay in the water, so the water is not soft: soap does not lather as freely and spotting can remain. It needs no salt, no brine drain and no electricity in most models <mark class="data-gap">[VERIFY by model]</mark>.

## Which Fits Salt Lake City Water?

If your [water tests](/water-quality-testing/) above 180 mg/L, choose salt-based, because conditioners are less effective as hardness rises. If your tap reads 121 to 180 mg/L and you want no salt or brine drain, a conditioner is a reasonable choice with a milder effect. For wells reading above 20 gpg, choose salt-based or a dual-tank system.

## Frequently Asked Questions

<details class="faq-item">
<summary>Does a salt-free softener really soften water?</summary>

No. It conditions water so scale forms less, but the hardness minerals stay in it. A salt-based softener removes them.

</details>

<details class="faq-item">
<summary>Is softened water safe to drink?</summary>

Softened water adds a small amount of sodium or potassium in proportion to the hardness removed. People on sodium-restricted diets should ask their doctor and can keep a drinking tap on unsoftened water.

</details>
